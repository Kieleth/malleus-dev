## Exact failure and what it teaches

The report claims that the average depth uncertainty is derived by comparing
earthquake depths across velocity models. In p7b009 the source instead compares
those depth shifts with an already reported average uncertainty. A comparison
against an uncertainty is not its derivation. The nearby error-ellipsoid method
in p7b005 does not establish how this exact average was obtained; p7b001 reports
a different mean vertical error in its own context. The averaging population is
also not established by saying the passage discusses axial events.

This is the observed semantic error, not a guess about model internals. The
quantity is not proved wrong, and DERIVED is unresolved rather than proved false.
Coarse computational status can be supportable while a particular determination
and its explanation are not. Matching paths, valid enum values and preserved
numbers cannot settle that distinction.

The new procedure makes the reasoning inspectable. All 46 report graph references
resolve to actual values; all 34 assertion references resolve in their declared
capture namespace; all nine new assertions contain complete blocks. Those checks
prevent false claims that fields exist and weakened evidence spans. They do not
prove that the text entails the proposed interpretation. One model-assisted
review detects this error; the paper guard mechanically enforces its exact
decision before retention. Neither is a guarantee of general source truth.

This run recovers useful context, including the previously missing depth datum.
It does not establish a causal improvement rate: target scope, context views,
evidence requirements and procedure changed together, with one producer sample.
No new task-coverage result follows from an unadmitted proposal.
