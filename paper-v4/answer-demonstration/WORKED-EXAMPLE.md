## What the replayed graph can answer

We examined two frozen captures of one marine geoscience article. Each ledger
reopened and reproduced its recorded graph before querying. Thirty
question-specific programs read stored values, relationships and provenance
through the public graph interface. No embedding index was used. The guarded
query phase recorded no source-file reads or network operations. This is a
bounded observation about these programs, not a comparison with retrieval
systems or an operating-system isolation guarantee.

One fresh model-assisted reviewer per capture examined every returned central
witness and every question against the selected PDF text layer. A third reviewer
independently rechecked six examples selected before review. Reviewers recorded
source support, coverage of each requested semantic, and whether answering rows
were linked. Human ratification remains pending. The queries were developed
against historical runs, so the experiment is retrospective.

The instrument-count query returned 19 deployed ocean-bottom seismometers from
each graph. Stored deployment scope distinguished this count from the 17
instruments used for arrival detection. One returned count lacked a subject
link, but its count_scope prose still identified the deployed network. Reading
that prose does not reconstruct a deployment event.

A spatial query returned an explicit relation between RC1 and its bounding
detachment fault in both graphs. In the second graph, following an existing
subject reference also recovered a claim describing the fault and core complex
on the eastern side. The relation was typed; the directional detail was stored
prose. We do not treat reading that prose as deriving a new spatial relation.

Both graphs preserved the authors' preferred explanation for deep earthquakes
as a hypothesis about CO2 degassing, with preference, modality and subject
available to the query. This is a representation of the authors' position,
not confirmation of the proposed mechanism.

Primary-melt queries returned six candidates per graph. The RC2 Ba-based
estimate retained its 0.4 to 3.0 wt% range and calculated status, while the
Rb-based estimate retained a distinct 0.5 to 2.8 wt% range. Reviewers found the
listed semantics covered through the explicitly scoped Ba-based row. Neither
the query nor the review collapsed these alternatives into a unique
proxy-independent answer.

The evidence-composition query returned the preferred mechanism but no SUPPORTS
path into it. The source explicitly relates the degassing calculation to deep
microseismicity. Both captures retained that passage, but neither population
proposed the corresponding support relationship. Replay preserved the submitted
graph; it did not supply the missing argument. The sulfur/chlorine control
returned no candidate. Reviewers independently checked the selected text and
found no requested concentration there, without extending that absence claim
to excluded graphics, supplements or the world.

Across all thirty questions, the preliminary reviews labelled eight fully
covered, fourteen partially covered and eight uncovered in the first run;
eleven, nine and ten respectively in the second. These counts include three
unavailable-information controls and two paraphrases. Coverage is not accuracy:
it can coexist with unresolved candidates or unlinked conditions. Neither run
fully covered any of the five composition questions. No model ranking follows
from two captures with different graphs and separate reviewers.

Review also exposed source-support errors within structurally admitted records.
One funding relation attached an award to the wrong funding attribution. One
negative claim omitted the source's restriction to observations available to
date. A separate review-input defect, missing definitions behind quantity enum
tokens, required a documented contextual rereading. Its amendments changed
witness support but no question-coverage count. Original reviews were retained.

These cases separate reproducible replay, source-supported representation and
retrieval with sufficient scope. Success at one does not establish the others.
The result is an inspectable document-to-graph demonstration with explicit
failures, not complete argument capture, prospective answer accuracy or evidence
that embedding-based retrieval is unnecessary for other documents or tasks.

## Separate query-only follow-up

We subsequently corrected two retrieval defects without changing either graph:
a literal-word restriction that excluded declined candidates, and text-layer
spacing and line-break forms that hid a stored caveat. Only three of sixty
question/run outputs changed; every relationship path was unchanged. A fresh
model-assisted subset review, with a separately retained correction of a
slot-only interpretation, found the second graph's caveat fully covered by
stored prose and its limitation field. The declined-mechanism outputs remained
partial and ambiguous, with no returned rejection ground. This separates a
retrieval improvement from a change in captured knowledge. It is not a new
complete review of all sixty outputs. QUERY-CORRECTION.md records the inputs,
review correction and interpretation limits.

## Fresh fixed-ontology population trial

A separate fresh Sol session received the same accepted ontology and reading,
updated generic adoption guidance and no questions or previous populations.
Its first submission admitted and replayed without structural feedback: 41
entities and five part-of relations. Its capture asserted material from fifteen
of the reading's 186 blocks. The producer explicitly described it as partial.
The unchanged query programs returned candidates for seven of thirty questions.
A fresh model-assisted review judged all six returned witnesses source-supported,
but no question fully covered: four were partial and twenty-six uncovered,
including controls. Human ratification remains pending. The result shows why
supported records and a reproducible ledger do not establish useful answers.

The trial exposes a retrieval assumption as well as incomplete capture. A CO2
quantity linked to a subject named primary melts was not reached by a program
requiring both expressions on the quantity record. Reproducible graph state
does not make a query insensitive to representation choices. Conversely, no
query traversal can recover a support edge that the producer did not supply.
This exploratory result changes model, guidance and acquisition context; it
does not isolate their effects or establish a model comparison.

A subsequent query-only pass followed explicit subject references during
candidate selection. It recovered the stored primary-melt quantity without
duplicating its subject name on the quantity record. Only three outputs changed;
all graph and ledger bytes and every relationship path were unchanged. A fresh
review found the quantity question and its paraphrase partially answered, but
the segment comparison still unanswered. The row carried a range, unit and
material, not the determination method or segment-specific context. This is a
bounded retrieval improvement, not a repaired capture or an inferred argument.
