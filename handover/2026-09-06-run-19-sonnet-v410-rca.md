## The cost of honesty, measured

Two questions PARTIAL for one structural reason: the records that answer them (the degassing claim with its PREFERRED disposition, the two deep-depth observations) carry no subject, and a subject-bearing record without a subject has no case in the binding since v4.4 (no ENTITY case) and no SUBJECT case without a subject. The answers are in the graph and unreachable. Run-16 reached them by anchoring, which the review punished; run-19 left them honest, which the binding punished.

The same measurement on every frozen cell at the stable Core (subject-bearing records with no subject whose type some set names): run-13 158, run-14 71, run-15 64, run-16 4, run-19 95. Reaching each as one ENTITY row per question that names its type adds up to that many rows per question: run-19 would go from 15, 37, 52, 50 to at most 68, 132, 147, 145; run-13 from 63, 127, 176, 149 to at most 133, 281, 334, 304. That is the flood v4.4 removed (1,466 rows in run-09), now one row per witness. Two ways to pay it, both rulings: reach every subject-bearing record as an ENTITY row (the binder drops the v4.4 restriction; the largest counts above), or reach only the subject-less ones (an executor rule that depends on a record's value, which the type-only binding has avoided by design; the counts above exactly). Not paying it means a producer that follows the subject rule honestly makes its best records invisible to the questions, which the paper cannot present as a feature.
