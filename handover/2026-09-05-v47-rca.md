## The finding: the comparison had no word boundary

Of the 33 attachable records (subject unset, exactly one capture entity named in a formalizing sentence, by the adapter's own rule) 14 name a tool, model or instrument and 17 a feature; several of the 17 are not what the record is about either. One of them attached a supplementary-data claim to the lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary because its tag LAB sits inside the word "available". The Core-15 comparison removes whitespace from both sides, case-folds, and tests substring. It cannot see word boundaries.

Measured with a bounded rule (the form's non-space characters in order with any whitespace between them, case-insensitive, not adjacent to a letter on either side; digits and punctuation are boundaries, so the text layer's glued citation numbers such as "lithosphere13" still name their entity and letter-spaced "R C 2" still matches):

| | substring (as shipped) | word-bounded |
|---|---|---|
| run-13 attachable / ambiguous / unnamed | 33 / 49 / 76 | 26 / 43 / 89 |
| run-12 projections that were in-word matches | | 8 of 15 |
| producer-set subjects refused, run-11 / 12 / 13 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 1 / 0 / 2 |

The eight run-12 projections were RTI inside "vertical" and "portion", OCC inside "occur", MAR inside "mark", OBS inside "observations". Of the seven that matched as a word, three attached a record to its software (HypoDD, SEISAN, NonLinLoc), one is marginal, three are plausible. The v4.6 RCA's sentence "a sentence naming exactly one entity names its tool as often as its subject" therefore rests on three or four cases, not thirteen; corrected in place and by E-0160. The three producer-set subjects a bounded rule would refuse are "axial valley" against "valleyﬂoor" (a ligature glued by the text layer, run-11) and "velocity model" against "velocity models" (twice, run-13): the cost of the rule is a tag for an inflected or glued form.

This closes option (b) from the iteration 4 reading as well: restricting projection to "about-able" entity types would not have helped, because after the in-word matches are removed the remaining wrong attachments are software named as a word, and the feature-typed attachments the reviewers demoted (a location error "about" the RTI, isotherm provenance "about" the thermal model) are named as words too. Named is not about; a type marker does not change that; only the producer's explicit subject and the review do.

## What the review found

Eighteen PARTIAL rows in 515, none UNSUPPORTED. Fifteen are aboutness findings on producer-set subjects: the subject's name is in the block and the sentence uses it as figure furniture, a locational reference, a direction marker, or the provenance of a figure's lines (the thermal model behind Figure 6's isotherms, five rows across two questions; the Romanche transform as a direction or a place, twice). Two are missing qualifiers, one is a semantic misreading of a solidus temperature. The second session stated the threshold it applied and that a stricter reading would demote about ninety locus-style rows; that is Luis's ruling at ratification.

Three harness debts the reviewers surfaced, in the order they cost accuracy:
1. Subtype re-projection. An ENTITY case for a type returns the records of its subtypes projected with the parent's fields. Software tools return under Instrument; three QuantityRatio records return under Ratio with Ratio's projection and so hide the digest their records carry; 30 of CQ-02's 127 rows re-project records already judged in the same question. The binder's v4.4 restriction (no ENTITY case for a subject-bearing type) is bypassed through a non-bearing parent. The elegant fix is one rule in the executor: a row projects the fields of the record's own type, and identical rows across cases of one question are one row.
2. Vocabulary: OPEN_LOWER_BOUND does not distinguish "at least" from "more than"; NEGATED was used on a hedged inference where HYPOTHESISED was available. The second is the queued item "hedged categorical values need a modality" seen from the review side.
3. A captured statement clipped one word before its predicate passes the verbatim check and the digest; only the review sees it.

Progression on every axis the cell measures: no return at either stage, coverage up, all four questions responsive, no UNSUPPORTED row, every digest verified, the census producing the attachable count instead of the overseer counting it. The one number that looks worse, 49 ambiguous against run-12's 5, is the substring rule's own artefact: run-12's producer set more subjects, so fewer records were classified at all, and the in-word matches inflate both attachable and ambiguous.
